# Stage 4 — Methodology & Evaluation Design

## Purpose
Specify a scientifically defensible way to answer the question with the resources
actually available or credibly planned. The output is a plan, not executed research.

## Procedure
1. Connect question → required evidence → resources → procedures → evaluation →
   interpretation. Identify dependencies and unresolved upstream assumptions.
2. Compare relevant methods, including those reconstructed in Stage 2. Explain
   adoption/adaptation and assumptions. Do not choose methods by familiarity alone.
3. Define ordered work packages with inputs, operations, intended outputs, decision
   points, and checks. Write declarative procedures, not executable research code.
4. Specify appropriate controls, comparators, validation and quality checks. Select
   the relevant domain profile; do not impose p-values, AUC, or sample size formulas
   on research types where they are inapplicable.
5. Specify interpretation rules, including null, negative, inconclusive, and adverse
   findings. Evidence quality, not a desired finding, defines success.
6. Address risks and alternatives: bias, leakage, uncertainty, measurement limits,
   reproducibility, reflexivity, proof assumptions, sensitivity/robustness as applicable.
7. Separate exploratory choices from prespecified confirmatory choices. For adaptive
   designs, specify change triggers and decision rules prospectively where possible.
8. Seek confirmation of method selection, evaluation and interpretive boundaries.

## Include in the specification
- Stepwise research procedure and dependency map.
- Comparison/validation, quality requirements, stopping/revision conditions.
- Expected artifact types (not invented results), reproducibility/documentation plan.
- Link to supporting evidence, decisions, resource prerequisites and open issues.

## Gate
Methods fit the question, resource conditions, and intended inference; material
uncertainty is declared. A proposed new method needs a validation strategy, not a
fabricated precedent. Tests or pilot analyses that require execution are handed off
as future tasks; they are not run under ResearchFlow.
