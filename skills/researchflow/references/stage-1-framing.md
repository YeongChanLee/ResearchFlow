# Stage 1 — Research Framing

## Purpose
Co-design what the research should establish, for whom, and within which boundaries.
An exploratory, interpretive, design-oriented, or theoretical inquiry may have an
objective rather than a fixed directional hypothesis.

## Procedure
1. Recover the researcher's motivation, existing work, known constraints, and prior
   decisions from the conversation. Distinguish supplied facts from assumptions.
2. Restate the problem and a tractable primary question. Separate primary objectives
   from optional extensions. Define scope and explicit non-goals.
3. Identify research mode: exploratory, confirmatory, descriptive, predictive,
   causal, experimental, qualitative, theoretical, or mixed (multiple may apply).
4. Discuss the desired contribution and what evidence could answer the question.
   Do not promise novelty before checking references; label novelty provisional.
5. List constraints: time, budget, data/material access, expertise, ethics, privacy,
   compute, and available tools. Avoid recording private details unnecessarily.
6. Offer a compact framing proposal and alternatives; seek explicit confirmation.

## Update Stage 1 of research_spec.md
- Motivation and primary question/objective; hypotheses only where appropriate.
- Research mode, intended contribution, context/units of inquiry.
- Scope, non-goals, constraints, assumptions and evidence requirements.
- Decisions still needed and scope of confirmation.

## Gate
The researcher confirms the question/objective and scope. A precise statistical
estimand is not universally required at this stage. No confirmation is inferred
from a request to keep drafting. Unverified feasibility remains explicitly open.
